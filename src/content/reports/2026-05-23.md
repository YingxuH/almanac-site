## The Future of Software Engineering

### Executive Summary
Building directly on yesterday's transition toward agent-first infrastructure, today marks the definitive arrival of the "parallel agent" era in local developer environments. Driven by a massive permanent price drop for DeepSeek's frontier models and the launch of multi-agent IDEs like YC-backed Superset, the economic and tooling barriers to running autonomous coding swarms have effectively vanished. However, as developers shift to managing parallel agents rather than writing code sequentially, researchers have exposed severe new "domain-camouflaged" injection vulnerabilities that exploit these exact multi-agent architectures, keeping security sentiment highly negative.

### Today's Top Signals

**1. Launch of Superset (YC P26): The Open-Source Agentic IDE**
Created by engineers Avi, Kiet, and Satya, Superset is a new open-source IDE built specifically to run agents like Claude Code, Codex, and OpenCode in parallel. This signals a critical UI/UX paradigm shift for the 3-10 year outlook: developers are transitioning from prompting a single sequential copilot to acting as a dispatcher for multiple concurrent AI workers triaging different parts of a codebase.

**2. DeepSeek v4 Pro API Permanent 75% Discount**
DeepSeek officially made its promotional discount permanent, reducing the cost of its frontier deepseek-v4-pro model to just 1/4 of its original price. For the 3-5 year outlook, this relentless downward pricing pressure on API calls is the primary economic catalyst enabling continuous, background multi-agent workflows, forcing Western labs into a rapid race to the bottom on token economics.

**3. Domain-Camouflaged Injection Attacks in Multi-Agent Systems**
A new arXiv paper details how domain-camouflaged injection attacks can successfully evade detection when passed between multiple interacting LLM agents. As the 5-10 year outlook relies heavily on agentic swarms (like those in Superset or Kanbots), lateral prompt-injection vulnerabilities between trusted agents represent a catastrophic systemic risk that current security paradigms cannot yet mitigate.

**4. The Rise of Agent-Native Workflow Tooling (Kanbots.dev)**
An open-source desktop Kanban application launched today that natively assigns and runs parallel AI agents on individual task cards. Over the next decade, project management and issue-tracking software will cease to be passive ledgers for human productivity and will transform into active execution environments where tickets resolve themselves.

**5. AI as a Multiplier for Pre-Existing Technical Skills**
Engineering commentary today strongly coalesced around AI functioning as a force multiplier for deep, pre-existing technical knowledge rather than a sheer replacement for it. For the 5-15 year labor outlook, this dampens the narrative that AI will completely level the playing field, suggesting instead that senior engineers capable of architectural oversight will see their output (and value) scale exponentially.

**6. Formal Verification of Apple Corecrypto**
Apple published a blueprint for the formal verification of its corecrypto module. While not directly an AI signal, the long-term relevance is stark: as AI generates exponentially more code of varying quality, the most critical foundational systems (like cryptography) are being secured via strict mathematical proofs rather than relying on human or AI-based unit testing.
